Florida man drills holes to drain rainwater from boat, sinks it at dock

He cut a second opening after the first let lake water in.

A Florida man drilled through the bottom of his fishing boat to drain rainwater, then cut a second hole as lake water poured in. The 14-foot boat was tied to a dock after an overnight storm had left several inches of water inside. He used a cordless drill and a two-inch hole saw near the stern, where the water had collected.

The first cut opened below the lake’s surface. When water began rising around his shoes, he decided the drain needed an air vent. “It can’t empty if it can’t breathe,” he said. Standing on a bench to keep the drill dry, he cut another hole a foot from the first. That opening was also below the waterline.

He climbed onto the dock as the boat settled stern-first. The dock lines held it in place until the bench disappeared beneath the water. He left the drill on the dock beside the two circular pieces of hull he had removed.
